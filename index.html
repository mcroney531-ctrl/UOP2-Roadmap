Design context: This is the original proposed roadmap for an instructional design initiative to develop platform support resources. The six-phase timeline below preserves the original planned sequence and June–July dates; it does not represent the full duration of the project. Production, review, and delivery continued beyond that initial window. Independent preparation, a live facilitator discovery session, and survey responses informed the work.

Why I adapted ADDIE: Rather than treating ADDIE as a rigid sequence, I kept the initial discovery phase focused on identifying the most important learner needs and content gaps. I began developing high-confidence resources while continuing to refine the content map, using facilitator feedback throughout the process to keep the work moving without sacrificing accuracy.

From discovery questions to content priorities: Alongside facilitator conversations and survey responses, I used an AI-assisted walkthrough to investigate the platform. Together, these inputs helped me develop roughly 60–70 working questions. I grouped and reframed smaller “micro-questions” into broader questions that individual support articles could address, then prioritized the work by audience need and topic: Priority 1 — Fundamentals (user-oriented basics, such as “Getting Started”); Priority 2 — Extensions (what users could do once they understood the basics); and Priority 3 — Support (troubleshooting and help). These are the later article-production priorities, separate from the six planned roadmap phases below.

Subsequent project leadership and production: After the original roadmap window, I continued leading the project through these three content-production phases. I assigned articles across the team, wrote some myself, and reviewed and refined others. At our first meeting, I established shared expectations for article structure, formatting, and language. We followed a recurring cycle of drafting, submission, refinement, and team discussion before moving to the next content phase. Throughout the work, I returned to facilitator discussion and survey input to check distinctions and maintain alignment; AI-assisted work occasionally resurfaced useful references.

Discovery examples — clarifying audience and scope: An article about creating groups initially needed a clearer audience: was it explaining the task from an administrator’s perspective or an end user’s? We narrowed and clarified the guidance rather than creating separate versions. In another case, I collaborated with a partner-facing colleague to define what “troubleshooting and support” actually needed to cover; we broke that broad topic into smaller, more focused pieces of guidance. Both examples reflect how stakeholder input shaped practical content decisions.

Continuing guide design and team enablement: I am leading the next stage of segmented, behavior-targeted in-product guidance and helping colleagues who are new to the guidance platform learn how to use it. I created a simple PowerPoint guide template, including instructions, so the team could plan guides in a familiar format. The intended workflow uses shared-file comments and review in SharePoint before transferring guide content into the in-product platform, rather than introducing a separate custom review tool. The guides remain in development, not live.

Quality review, technical integration, and next steps: The team developed approximately 15 support articles and completed two rounds of facilitator/subject-matter expert accuracy review. The articles support a retrieval-augmented knowledge source intended for on-demand user assistance; Priority 3 support articles are still being processed by the technical team, so integration is not yet complete across all priorities. The content is also informing segmented, behavior-targeted in-product guides, which remain in development and are not live. It is too early to assess user adoption, effectiveness, or learning-performance outcomes.

Phase 1
Discovery
Jun 14–19
Key Activities
  • Independent platform orientation and question preparation before facilitator conversations
  • Live facilitator discovery session completed — its input became a recurring reference throughout design
  • Facilitator survey distributed and responses received — findings informed design alongside the live session (individual results not reproduced here)
  • Content inventory — catalog what exists on the support site and platform
  • Review existing video assets for extractable language
  • Synthesize findings across conversations, survey responses, and inventory exploration into a rough map and a short list of high-confidence build candidates
⏱️
Hard Cap Mindset
The output of this phase isn't completeness — it's a rough map of what exists, the obvious gaps, and 1–2 high-confidence things worth building. If the week ends and you're still "figuring it out," it's gone too deep.
Phase 2
Design + Build Start
Jun 22–24
Key Activities
  • Content mapping — what exists, what needs to be built, where things link or extend
  • Categorize content as: Link (point to existing), Companion (extend existing), or New Build
  • Bring 2–3 tiered directions early in the phase
  • In parallel: begin building on anything clearly Safe or Stretch — drafts, outlines, page structure
  • Early draft of mapping due — Jun 26
  • EOP Target a largely complete map, and at least one real asset in draft form — not just planned.
↓ Tiered Ambition Framework — applied in this phase

Before bringing content ideas to review, each will be categorized by ambition level. This prevents good ideas from being self-filtered out before leadership has a chance to weigh in — and provides a menu, not just a single recommendation.

🟢 Safe — existing content, light lift, clear precedent
🔵 Stretch — new content, moderate build, fits expected scope
🟣 Bold — new format, feature, or approach; potentially requiring in-product guidance or technical integration

Phases 3–4
Design / Dev Blend

Key Activities
  • Lean into production while continuing to refine the map
  • Continue building content pages and assets
  • Run lightweight review loops as things take shape — not formal sign-off cycles
  • Keep facilitator discussion references and survey input available to check distinctions and maintain alignment during development
  • Fill in remaining gaps in the map only where they affect what's actively being built
🔁
Jun 26 Checkpoint — Midpoint, Not Final
The Jun 26 deliverable was planned as a midpoint. The standard: direction is solid, builds are underway. By end of this phase, most core assets should exist in draft form.
Phase 5
QA & Review
Jul 2–3
Key Activities
  • Consistency, tone, and accuracy pass across all content
  • Facilitator QA — loop in trusted facilitators to flag anything that doesn't reflect how the platform actually works
  • Final sign-off before handoff to tech team
  • Revision cleanup — address feedback before pages are considered ready for implementation
✅
Final Reviews
By this point, review has already been happening throughout Phases 2–4 — this phase confirms everything is ready, not where direction gets decided. Focus is consistency and accuracy. If you're still debating what to build here, you're behind.
Phase 6
Implementation
Jul 6
Key Activities
  • Finalize and package content for handoff
  • Hand off completed pages to tech team for platform integration
  • Coordinate on technical requirements and page structure ahead of handoff
  • Validate links, structure, and placement within the platform
  • Deployment, not creation — the content already exists by this point. The target was to have materials ready for the planned July 6 implementation handoff.
After handoff
Evaluation
Ongoing
Key Activities
  • Confirm pages are live and functioning within the platform
  • Gather initial feedback from facilitators and users
  • Collect live feedback from learners and maintain ongoing assessment of evolving needs
  • Iterate based on real use — adjust language, structure, or content as needed
Tiered Ambition Framework
The Tiered Ambition Framework
Applied in Phase 2 · Design

The roadmap calls for assigning each content idea a tier before review:

Safe
  • Existing content or a light-lift adaptation with clear precedent.
🟢Looks like
  • Linking to an existing support page already covering the topic
  • Repurposing language from a vetted video into a written page
  • A companion page that extends something already built, staying close to the original
  • A feature explainer that mirrors the platform's own existing documentation
Stretch
  • New content that doesn't exist yet but fits comfortably within expected scope. Moderate build effort; the idea itself isn't a reach, just the execution.
🔵Looks like
  • Creating a platform walkthrough
  • Drafting a reference page that doesn't exist anywhere currently
  • Building out an FAQ page
Bold
  • Genuinely new in format, feature, or approach. Outside established precedent. Requires leadership sign-off before investing in. potentially requiring in-product guidance or technical integration ideas.
🟣Looks like
  • Embedding an interactive element within the platform page itself (e.g. a "?" help trigger)
  • Placing content inside the platform UI rather than on a support page
  • Introducing a format or content type that hasn't been done before in this context
